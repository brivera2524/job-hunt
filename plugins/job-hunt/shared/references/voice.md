# Writing in the user's voice

Application answers, cover letters and outreach drafts must read like the user wrote them on a good day: plain, specific, conversational, technically accurate. Reviewers increasingly skim past anything that sounds machine-written.

## The approved sample

During setup the user approves a short sample answer (stored at the top of `settings/answers`). Match its sentence length, formality and vocabulary. If the user edits a draft, treat the edit as the new standard and update `settings/answers`.

## Do

- Lead with the concrete thing: what they built, checked, fixed or ran, with the real number when the dossier has one.
- Use first person and short sentences. One idea per sentence.
- Name the company's actual product or problem from the posting, briefly, then connect it to one or two dossier facts.
- Admit a gap plainly when a question asks about it ("I haven't used Kubernetes in production yet; it's the area I'd grow into first").
- Keep "why us" answers to 60–120 words unless the form asks for more.

## Don't

- Em-dash asides, "not X, but Y" constructions, colon-then-reveal sentences, rhetorical questions.
- Stock phrases: "I'm passionate about", "I'm excited to", "leverage", "delve", "thrilled", "fast-paced environment", "proven track record", "results-driven", "unique blend", "I believe I would be a great fit".
- Restating the job description back to them.
- Claims the dossier doesn't support, even soft ones ("expert in", "extensive experience").
- Exclamation marks.

## Outreach drafts (referrals, recruiters)

Under 300 characters for a LinkedIn connection note; under 120 words for a message or email. Say who they are to the person (shared school, mutual connection, same field), which role they applied to or are considering, one concrete reason they fit, and a small ask (a quick chat, or pointing to the right person). The user sends it.
